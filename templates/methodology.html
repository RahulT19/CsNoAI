Methodology

Every number on the dashboard is produced by the four steps below. There is no trained model and no hidden state: given the same ten rows, the arithmetic here reproduces the forecast exactly, by hand if you like.

1. Extraction

A single static quote page is requested per symbol with requests, behind a desktop User-Agent. BeautifulSoup(html, "html.parser") builds the DOM; the price table is identified by its header labels, and the ten most recent sessions are read out of the <tr> and <td> nodes. Corporate action rows — dividends and splits — carry no OHLC data and are skipped.

No JSON endpoint, no CSV download and no API wrapper is used, and the scraper never follows a link. If the live page is blocked or renders its table client-side, an offline HTML snapshot is parsed by the same function, so the extraction path is identical either way.

2. Structuring

pandas coerces the scraped strings to numerics, drops sessions missing a high, low or close, de-duplicates repeated dates, repairs any row whose high prints below its low, sorts chronologically, clips to the trailing ten sessions, and attaches the ordinal index t = 1…n that the regression is fitted against.

3. Ordinary least squares

For each target series y — the daily high and the daily low, fitted independently — we minimise the residual sum of squares Σ(yᵢ − ŷᵢ)² for the line ŷ = m·x + c. Setting both partial derivatives to zero gives the normal equations, whose solution is: 

      n·Σ(xy) − Σx·Σy
m =  ──────────────────          c = ȳ − m·x̄
      n·Σ(x²) − (Σx)²

The implementation uses the equivalent matrix form on the design matrix X = [1 x], solving β = (XᵀX)⁻¹Xᵀy with np.linalg.solve rather than forming an explicit inverse. The test suite pins the result against both numpy.polyfit and the summation identity above to within 1e-9.

Goodness of fit

R² = 1 − SS_res / SS_tot = 1 − Σ(yᵢ − ŷᵢ)² / Σ(yᵢ − ȳ)²

σ̂ = √( SS_res / (n − 2) )      SE(m) = σ̂ / √Σ(xᵢ − x̄)²      t = m / SE(m)

With n = 10 there are 8 degrees of freedom, so |t| > 2.306 is significant at the 5% level. A perfectly flat series has no variance to explain; the engine reports R² = 0 there rather than dividing by zero.

The forecast

Both fitted lines are evaluated one step beyond the observed window, at t = 11, giving a projected trading band rather than a single point. Because the two models are independent they can cross on a converging series, so Ĥ ≥ L̂ is enforced before the values reach the decision layer.

4. The decision rules

Potential upside = (Ĥ₁₁ − Closeₜ) / Closeₜ
Downside risk    = (Closeₜ − L̂₁₁) / Closeₜ

Evaluated top to bottom — the first match wins:

The decision table, generated from the thresholds the engine actually runs on.
#ConditionVerdict
1 Downside ≥ {{ '%.1f'|format(thresholds.downside) }}% or slope(High) < 0 SELL
2 Upside ≥ {{ '%.1f'|format(thresholds.upside) }}% and R²(High) > {{ '%.2f'|format(thresholds.r2) }} and slope(High) > 0 BUY
3 anything else HOLD

Rule 1 is an override, not an alternative. A widening high–low spread can project a large upside and a large drawdown at the same time; testing the sell condition first means the engine structurally cannot recommend buying into that regime. Rule 2 carries two clauses beyond the raw upside: the R² floor demands that the trendline explains the recent path rather than fitting noise, and the positive-slope requirement stops the engine buying a falling knife whose projected high merely sits above a depressed close.

Where this breaks

  1. Linear extrapolation over ten sessions captures short-term drift and nothing else — no mean reversion, no volatility clustering, no gaps, no earnings dates. Its error grows without bound as the horizon extends; one session is the honest limit.
  2. A high R² is not predictive skill. It says the last ten points sat near a line, not that the eleventh will. That is exactly why the slope sign and the downside boundary act as independent gates.
  3. Ten observations is a very small sample. Two degrees of freedom go to the fit, leaving eight — enough for a t-test, not enough for confidence in the tails.
  4. The offline corpus is realistically shaped but synthetic. It guarantees the demo runs; it is not a live market record.
  5. There are no transaction costs, no slippage, no position sizing and no backtest. The signal is a directional opinion, not a trading system.
